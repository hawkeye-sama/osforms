The alternatives that come up most in developer roundups are Formcarry, Basin, Web3Forms, FormSubmit, Netlify Forms, Formgrid, and OSForms. They split on three questions: how many free submissions you get, which integrations sit behind a paywall, and whether you can leave with your data. If you want the most free volume for plain email delivery, Web3Forms (250/month) wins. If your site already lives on Netlify, Netlify Forms costs nothing extra. If you want a backend you can self-host, OSForms and Formgrid are the two open-source options, and OSForms is the one with Google Sheets, webhooks, and auto-replies on the free tier.

I built OSForms, so read this as a biased source and check the links. Every competitor number below comes from that vendor's own pricing page or docs, checked in November 2026, and each section says when that tool is the better pick.

## Why People Look for Formspree Alternatives

Formspree is a solid product. It has been around for over a decade, the docs are mature, and the core loop (point your form at a URL, get an email) works. People leave because of where [Formspree's plans page](https://formspree.io/plans) puts the walls (November 2026):

- **50 submissions a month free**, with a 30-day submission archive. A lead from six weeks ago is gone.
- **Google Sheets and CSV export start at Personal**, $15/month billed monthly ($10/month billed yearly), which also only raises the cap to 200.
- **Webhooks, auto-response, and Zapier start at Professional**, $30/month billed monthly ($20/month billed yearly), with 2,000 submissions.
- **The code went closed.** Formspree began as an open-source project, but the [repo was archived](https://github.com/formspree/formspree) in July 2023 and the README says the hosted product's source is not available.

So the useful comparison axes are free volume, which plan integrations start on, data retention, and whether there is an exit. That is what the table above scores.

## OSForms: All Integrations Free on Your Own Keys

OSForms is the open-source form backend I built around one idea: you bring your own API keys, so the integrations cost nothing to run and nothing to use. The hosted free tier is 100 submissions a month, and nothing on it is gated:

- **Email notifications and auto-replies** through your own [Resend](https://resend.com) key.
- **Google Sheets** through your own Google account, using the narrow `drive.file` OAuth scope. New form fields become new columns automatically. The [Google Sheets walkthrough](/form-to-google-sheets) shows the setup.
- **Webhooks** to any URL, with an optional HMAC-SHA256 signature.
- **Spam protection** with a honeypot field you name yourself, plus reCAPTCHA or hCaptcha on your own keys.
- **No expiry and CSV export on every tier**, plus a read-only pull API (`osf_live_` keys) if you want submissions in your own code.

Keys are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM and decrypted only when an integration runs. The [whole platform is open source](https://github.com/hawkeye-sama/osforms) under MIT, and self-hosting runs the same Next.js and MongoDB codebase on your own infrastructure. There the monthly limit is a field on your user record in your own database: it still defaults to 100, so raise it after setup. The reasoning behind the model is in [what BYOK means for a form backend](/bring-your-own-key), and [why your form tool shouldn't own your data](/own-your-form-data) covers the retention and export side.

**Pick something else if:** you need file uploads (OSForms stores a placeholder string like `[File: resume.pdf, 48210 bytes]`, not the file), you want one-click plugins for Slack, Mailchimp, or Notion, or you need more than 100 hosted submissions a month and won't self-host.

If you are deciding between Formspree and OSForms specifically, the feature-by-feature version is in [OSForms vs Formspree](/osforms-vs-formspree). If it's Typeform you are replacing, see the [open source Typeform alternative](/typeform-alternative) for the conversational React component.

## Web3Forms: The Biggest Free Email Tier

[Web3Forms' pricing page](https://web3forms.com/pricing) (November 2026) lists a free plan with **250 submissions a month**, unlimited forms, hCaptcha, honeypot, custom redirects, and CSV export. You add a hidden `access_key` input to your form and submissions are emailed to you. The free dashboard shows the last 30 days of submission history.

Integrations are where it tiers. Webhooks, Google Sheets, Slack, autoresponders, and file uploads are marked as paid features. Pro is **$149 a year** (shown as $12/month billed yearly) for 10,000 submissions, and there is a $49/year Starter plan for students, early startups, and nonprofits.

**Pick Web3Forms if:** you only need submissions in your inbox and want the most free volume. For a static portfolio or landing page, 250 a month with no integrations is a good deal, and it beats OSForms on raw free volume.

## Formcarry: Cheapest Paid Entry With File Uploads

[Formcarry's pricing page](https://formcarry.com/pricing) (November 2026) has a free Baby plan with **1 form and 50 submissions a month**. Paid plans start low: Starter is $6/month ($5 billed yearly) for unlimited forms, 500 submissions, 1GB of file uploads, and 500 integration calls a month. Basic is $19/month for 2,000 submissions.

Its [integrations page](https://formcarry.com/integrations) lists Google Sheets, Airtable, Mailchimp, Notion, Slack, Discord, and Twilio, and the pricing FAQ mentions webhooks and a REST API. Integration usage is metered per plan in "integration calls," so check your expected volume against that quota.

**Pick Formcarry if:** you need file uploads on a budget, or you want a cheap paid plan with a wider native integration catalog than OSForms has. $6 a month for 500 submissions and real file storage is a fair price.

## Basin: Long Retention and File Storage on Paid Plans

[Basin's pricing page](https://usebasin.com/pricing) (November 2026) lists a free plan with **1 form endpoint, 50 submissions a month, 30-day data retention**, 100MB of file uploads, and a Zapier integration. Starter is $12.50/month billed yearly for 250 submissions and 365-day retention.

Google Sheets, webhooks, and auto-responses first appear on the **Growth plan, $24.17/month billed yearly**, which also brings 1,000 submissions and unlimited retention.

**Pick Basin if:** you are willing to pay and want a hosted backend with file storage and Zapier from day one. It is a mature product with a clear upgrade path. On the free tier it covers about the same ground as Formspree's.

## Netlify Forms: Free if You Already Host on Netlify

Netlify Forms is different from everything else here: it is part of a hosting platform, not a standalone endpoint. You add `data-netlify="true"` to a form, and [Netlify detects it when it builds your site](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/forms/setup/). That build-time detection is also the catch, since it is built for sites deployed on Netlify.

On Netlify's credit-based plans, the [billing FAQ](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/billing-faq-for-credit-based-plans/) says form submissions are **free and unlimited**. One caveat: if a team runs out of credits, its projects pause and stop receiving form submissions. Notifications go to email, Slack, or a webhook, and submissions can be downloaded as CSV. Email notifications must go to a member of your Netlify team unless you are on an Enterprise plan.

**Pick Netlify Forms if:** your site already deploys on Netlify. Adding a second form vendor makes little sense when submissions are included. Look elsewhere if you host on Vercel, Cloudflare Pages, or GitHub Pages, or render forms client-side and don't want to add the hidden-form workaround.

## FormSubmit: Zero Signup, Email Only

[FormSubmit](https://formsubmit.co/) is the lightest option. There is no registration: set your form's action to `https://formsubmit.co/` followed by your email address, confirm the address once, and submissions arrive by email. It supports `_autoresponse`, `_webhook`, `_next` for a thank-you redirect, and three email templates. reCAPTCHA is on by default. The homepage describes the service as free and lists no paid plans.

The tradeoff is that the homepage describes email delivery only, with no dashboard or export, so plan on your inbox being the archive. The `_autoresponse` option also does not work with AJAX submissions or with reCAPTCHA turned off.

**Pick FormSubmit if:** you want a contact form working in sixty seconds and don't need storage, export, or Sheets.

## Formgrid: The Other Open-Source Option

[Formgrid](https://formgrid.dev) is the one other tool in this list you can run yourself. Its site says it is "fully open source under the MIT license," and the [repository](https://github.com/allenarduino/formgrid) ships a Docker Compose setup for self-hosting, which its pricing page describes as free with unlimited usage.

The hosted [pricing page](https://formgrid.dev/pricing) (November 2026) lists a free plan with **50 submissions a month across up to 3 forms**, email notifications, and a one-time Google Sheets sync of 10 rows. Premium is $12/month for 1,000 submissions, autoresponders, CSV export, file uploads up to 1GB per file, and 500 Sheets rows a month. Webhooks and Zapier are listed on Business at $29/month, with 15,000 submissions.

**Pick Formgrid if:** you want open source and the option to self-host, but also a cheap paid hosted plan with file uploads, which OSForms doesn't offer today. On the hosted free tier the two differ: OSForms gives you 100 submissions with ongoing Sheets sync, webhooks, and auto-replies on your own keys, where Formgrid's free plan gives you 50 and a 10-row Sheets trial.

## How to Choose a Formspree Alternative

Match the tool to the one thing you can't live without:

- **Most free volume, email only:** Web3Forms.
- **Already on Netlify:** Netlify Forms.
- **No account at all:** FormSubmit.
- **File uploads on a small budget:** Formcarry.
- **Paid hosted backend with long retention and Zapier:** Basin or Formspree.
- **Open source with a cheap paid hosted plan and file uploads:** Formgrid.
- **Sheets, webhooks, and auto-replies free, open source, self-hostable:** OSForms.

Switching from Formspree to any endpoint-based tool here is mostly a one-attribute change. With OSForms it looks like this:

```html
<!-- Before -->
<form action="https://formspree.io/f/xyzabcde" method="POST">
  <!-- your fields -->
</form>

<!-- After -->
<form action="https://osforms.com/api/v1/f/abc123xyz" method="POST">
  <!-- same fields, same names -->
</form>
```

Field names carry over because these services store whatever your inputs are named. Two steps to do in order: export your Formspree submissions first (CSV export starts on its Personal plan, and the free archive only covers 30 days), then reconnect integrations on the new side before you swap the URL. If you use `fetch`, OSForms returns `{ "success": true }` for JSON requests; plain HTML forms can set a redirect URL and get a 303 to your thank-you page. The [two-minute setup guide](/form-backend-setup) covers the rest.
