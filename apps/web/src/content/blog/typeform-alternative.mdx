The open source Typeform alternative for React developers is OSForms: an MIT-licensed form backend plus `@osforms/react`, a component that renders the same one-question-at-a-time flow inside your own app, with 100 free submissions a month and integrations on your own API keys. [Typeform's pricing page](https://www.typeform.com/pricing/) starts paid plans at $39 a month for 100 responses, and its [help center](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032972852-Free-plan) puts the free plan at 10 responses a month. If you want a full open-source form builder with a drag-and-drop editor instead of a component, Formbricks, OpnForm, and HeyForm are the ones to look at, compared further down.

Typeform's one-question-at-a-time flow is good, and plenty of people pay for it happily. The friction shows up on the pricing page and on the day you want your data somewhere else. I build OSForms, so treat this as a biased source and check the specifics: every Typeform figure below links to Typeform's own pages, checked in November 2026.

## Why Look for a Typeform Alternative

People rarely leave Typeform because the product is bad. They leave because of the shape of the deal. Three walls come up again and again.

The first is the **price of entry**. The free plan's 10 responses a month is enough to try the builder, not to run a contact form. The first paid tier is Basic at $39 a month. For a marketing team running one flagship survey, $39 is a rounding error. For a developer who wants a decent contact form on a side project, or anyone testing an idea before it has traction, paying $39 a month to receive form fills is a strange place to start.

The second is **where the data lives**. Every answer sits on Typeform's servers, under Typeform's account, exported on Typeform's terms. That is fine until you need the raw responses in your own database, or a client asks where their leads are stored, or a GDPR question lands on your desk and the only answer is "a vendor has it." (The longer version of that argument is in [why your form tool shouldn't own your data](/own-your-form-data).)

The third is **the embed**. Typeform's [embed SDK](https://github.com/Typeform/embed), React wrapper included, drops an iframe or a hosted widget into your page. It renders, it works, and it is still their form running inside your site. You do not control the markup, the submission handling, or what happens to the payload after someone hits submit.

All three follow from how a hosted form builder makes money, and the alternatives below are built on different models.

For a developer who wants a nice contact form on a portfolio, or an agency that needs a lead form on forty client sites, the math gets ugly fast: forty client sites at the entry tier is over $1,500 a month just to receive form submissions.

With OSForms, if you outgrow the hosted tier, you self-host the open-source version and the cap becomes a setting in your own database. No per-response meter, and no plan that gates the ability to send an email when someone fills out your form.

## Where Typeform Is Still the Right Choice

Typeform is the better pick in three cases, and if you are in any of them, stop reading and go pay them.

**You are not a developer.** Typeform's builder lets you design the whole form in a browser, no code, and non-technical teammates can edit it later. OSForms has a dashboard builder too, but the conversational component shines brightest when you are comfortable in a React codebase. If nobody on the team writes code, Typeform (or Tally) removes a real barrier.

**You need complex branching.** Typeform's Logic feature can jump a respondent to a completely different question based on an answer, run calculations, and pipe earlier answers into later question text. OSForms supports conditional logic that shows or hides fields based on previous answers, which covers a lot of real cases, but it is not full branch-to-another-path logic yet. If your survey is a decision tree, Typeform is stronger.

**You want the template gallery and the polish out of the box.** Typeform has years of design work, a large template library, and integrations with hundreds of tools through its own directory. OSForms gives you three integrations (email, Sheets, webhooks) on your own keys, plus a clean default theme. If you want to pick a pre-made quiz template and launch in ten minutes with zero code, that is Typeform's home turf.

## The Open Source Typeform Alternative: @osforms/react

The reason a form backend can replace Typeform at all is `@osforms/react`. It is a small React package that renders a full form from a schema, and its default mode is conversational: one field per screen, animated transitions, a progress bar, Enter to advance, and auto-advance on choice fields. Welcome and thank-you screens, conditional fields, and theming are all configured in the schema or through props.

The smallest working version is one tag:

```tsx
import { OSForm } from '@osforms/react';

export default function Contact() {
  // Conversational by default; pass mode="classic" for a single-page layout.
  return <OSForm formId="your-form-slug" />;
}
```

The component fetches your form's schema, renders the flow, and posts the answers to your OSForms endpoint. The full build, including the DIY state machine it replaces, a schema defined in code, the props reference, and the welcome and thank-you screens, is in [how to add a conversational form to React](/conversational-form-react). If you are new to wiring form state in React generally, [how to handle forms in React](/forms-in-react) covers the fundamentals.

> "Typeform rents you a beautiful form. An open-source conversational form is one you own outright: the code, the responses, and the keys behind every integration."

## Other Open Source Typeform Alternatives

OSForms is not the only open-source answer, and depending on who builds your forms it may not be the right one. The three projects below are full form builders: a visual editor, hosted form pages, and a results dashboard, which you can self-host. OSForms is a React component plus a form backend. If your forms are built by a marketing team in a browser, start with one of these. If they live in a React codebase, OSForms fits better.

The license difference matters if you plan to modify and host the code for others: AGPL requires you to make your modified source available to the people who use it over a network, and MIT does not.

## Own the Responses and the Keys

With OSForms, every answer posts to an endpoint you control, gets stored as JSON you can export to CSV whenever you like, and routes to integrations running on your own credentials. Want an email on every submission? Add your Resend key. Want rows in a spreadsheet? Connect your own Google account. Want it in your database? Point a signed webhook at your API. None of that sits behind a plan tier, because OSForms is not reselling you access to those services. That is what [bring your own key](/bring-your-own-key) means in practice, and it is why the integrations are free.

The response cap question also disappears if you want it to. The hosted tier is 100 submissions a month, which is plenty for most forms. But the platform is open source, so when a form takes off, you clone the repo, point it at your own MongoDB, and raise the monthly limit on your user record (it defaults to 100) to whatever your infrastructure can handle.

This is the same argument I make for [any form backend versus a hosted tool](/handle-form-submissions): the tool that owns your data and hides its code is the tool you are renting from, on terms that can change. If you want the Typeform feel without the Typeform deal, create a form, install `@osforms/react`, and render it.
